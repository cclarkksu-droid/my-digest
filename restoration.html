The SL Restorer

Learn to restore a classic Mercedes-Benz SL 500 — one weekend at a time

The Curriculum

A self-paced path from total beginner to confident weekend restorer.

Which "vintage SL 500" do you have? Two generations wear the badge. The W107 "500 SL" (1980–1989) is the chrome-bumpered icon — simple, mechanical, very restorable. The R129 "SL 500" (1990–2002) is more modern — hydraulic soft top, early electronics, and a few famous wiring gremlins. This app covers both; issue and spec pages are split by chassis.

Module 1 — Restoration Mindset Start here

Restoration is 20% wrenching and 80% patience, research, and organization. Before touching a bolt:

  • Decide your goal: concours show car, clean driver, or "rolling restoration" you enjoy while improving. For a first project, aim for a driver-quality car — perfection triples the cost.
  • Buy the best car you can afford. A $10k rust-free car beats a $4k rusty one every time. Rust repair is the great budget-killer.
  • Document everything. Photograph every assembly before disassembly. Bag and label every fastener. Future-you will be grateful.
  • One system at a time. Never have more than one system apart at once, or the car becomes a parts pile.

Module 2 — Buying & Assessing an SL

The 30-minute inspection walk
  • Rust points (W107): floor pans, bumper filler panels, windshield frame base, jack points, subframe mounts, spare wheel well, fender arches behind the trim.
  • Rust points (R129): better protected, but check jack points, front fenders behind the wheel liners, and under the soft-top well drains.
  • Match the numbers: the data card (in the trunk or under the hood) lists paint code, options, and engine number. Verify against the VIN.
  • Cold start it. Listen for timing-chain rattle (first 2 seconds), lifter tick, and watch for blue smoke on the first rev.
  • Operate everything: soft top, hardtop fit, windows, climate control. On an R129, a dead soft-top hydraulic system is a $1–3k line item.
Paperwork that adds (or subtracts) thousands
  • Service history binder — stamps or invoices. A documented car is worth 15–30% more.
  • Original books, tool roll, first-aid kit, and both tops (W107 hardtop + soft top) present.
  • Check for accident history and mismatched paint with a cheap paint-depth gauge (~$30).

Module 3 — Workshop Fundamentals

Safety first, always
  • Never work under a car on a jack alone — jack stands on solid ground, always, plus a wheel chocked.
  • Disconnect the battery negative terminal before any electrical or fuel work.
  • Fuel, brake cleaner, and old gear oil: gloves, ventilation, and a proper fire extinguisher (ABC rated) within reach.
  • Penetrating oil today saves a snapped bolt tomorrow. Soak rusty fasteners the night before.
Reading factory documentation

Mercedes documentation is superb. Learn to use:

  • EPC (Electronic Parts Catalog) — exploded diagrams and part numbers for every assembly.
  • WIS (Workshop Information System) — the factory repair procedures, torque specs included.
  • The classic printed "Big Blue Books" service manuals for W107 if you prefer paper.
Fastener literacy
  • Mercedes is metric everywhere: 8, 10, 13, 17, 19 mm cover 90% of the car.
  • Learn torque-to-spec habits early; aluminum threads (valve covers, thermostat housings) strip easily.
  • Replace, don't reuse: stretch bolts, copper crush washers, self-locking nuts.

Module 4 — The Big Five Systems

1. Brakes (do this first)

Pads, rotors, flexible hoses, and a full fluid flush are the mandatory first job on any newly bought classic. Brake fluid is hygroscopic — if you don't know its age, it's too old. Practice bleeding brakes; it teaches hydraulics cheaply.

2. Cooling

V8 SLs run hot in traffic. Replace radiator hoses, thermostat, and coolant on schedule. On the M119 (R129), the plastic radiator neck goes brittle with age. Only use Mercedes-approved coolant (G05/blue) — the wrong coolant eats head gaskets.

3. Ignition & Fuel

The W107 500 SL uses Bosch K-Jetronic (mechanical fuel injection) — rebuildable, but learn its logic before adjusting anything. The R129 uses electronic LH-Jetronic then ME engine management. Old fuel hoses are a fire risk: replace all soft lines as preventive maintenance.

4. Suspension & Steering

Rubber ages even parked: bushings, ball joints, tie rods, and the steering damper transform how the car drives. This is the highest satisfaction-per-dollar job on an old SL.

5. Electrical

Start with a clean battery, clean grounds, and a multimeter. Most "gremlins" are corroded grounds or 40-year-old connectors. On 1992–95 R129s, inspect the engine wiring harness — the insulation was biodegradable and crumbles (see Known Issues).

Module 5 — Bodywork & Interior Basics

  • Paint correction before paint jobs: most "tired" paint revives with clay bar, polish, and wax. A respray is a last resort ($8–20k done right).
  • Rust triage: surface rust → sand, convert, prime. Perforation → cut and weld; this is where beginners should hire a professional and watch.
  • Leather revival: Mercedes leather responds beautifully to cleaning + re-dyeing kits. Wood trim can be re-lacquered by specialists.
  • Soft top: W107 tops are a satisfying (if fiddly) DIY replacement; R129 tops interact with the hydraulic frame — study first.

Module 6 — Community & Continuing Education

  • Join the SL-focused forums and your local Mercedes-Benz club — chapter tech days are free education.
  • Find a marque specialist shop before you need one, for the jobs above your level.
  • Keep a build thread (photos + notes). It doubles your resale value story and keeps you motivated.

Known Issues by Chassis

The problems every SL buyer and owner should know cold.

R129 SL 500 (1990–2002)

IssueSeverityWhat to know
Biodegradable engine wiring harness High 1992–1995 cars (M119 V8): insulation crumbles, causing misfires and can destroy the ECU. Inspect near the injectors; replacement harnesses are available. Also check the throttle actuator harness.
Soft-top hydraulic cylinders leak High Seals age out in all of them eventually. Green fluid stains in the top well or behind the seats. Cylinders can be resealed by specialists far cheaper than new.
M119 timing chain guides / oil tubes Medium Plastic guides embrittle; rattle at cold start warrants inspection. Lower valvetrain oil tubes pop off causing lifter tick — cheap fix, common misdiagnosis.
Throttle body / ASR actuator failure Medium Limp-home mode, cruise control dead. Rebuilt units available; often wiring-related on early cars.
Radiator neck & plastic cooling parts Medium Brittle with age; carry-a-spare territory on 25-year-old originals. Replace proactively during a cooling refresh.
Hardtop headliner sag, window regulators Low Cosmetic/annoyance items with well-documented DIY fixes.

W107 500 SL (1980–1989)

IssueSeverityWhat to know
Rust — floors, subframe mounts, windshield frame High The number-one value factor. Windshield-frame rust is costly (glass out, paint). Check under the battery tray and behind bumper fillers too.
Timing chain & guides (M117 V8) High Single-row chains on earlier engines and aged plastic guides. If history is unknown, budget a chain/guide/tensioner service — engine-saving insurance.
K-Jetronic fuel system aging Medium Warm-up regulator, fuel accumulator, and injector seals cause hard starts and rough running. Rebuildable; do not "adjust to fix" — diagnose with pressure gauges.
Climate control servo (early cars) Medium The infamous ACC servo leaks coolant and fails. Updated replacements exist.
Sagging soft top & hardtop seals Low Wear items; full seal kits available. Water leaks here cause the floor rust above.

Tools & Budget

What to buy, in what order, and what the money reality looks like.

Starter Tool Kit (~$600–900)

  • Metric socket set 1/4" + 3/8" drive (6-point), 8–19 mm; metric combination wrenches
  • Torque wrench (3/8", 10–100 Nm range), breaker bar, quality screwdriver set
  • 3-ton floor jack + two pairs of jack stands + wheel chocks
  • Multimeter, test light, battery maintainer
  • Brake bleeder (one-person pressure type), fluid catch pans, funnel set
  • Penetrating oil, anti-seize, dielectric grease, brake cleaner, nitrile gloves
  • Zip-lock bags + paint pen for fastener labeling; phone tripod for teardown photos

Level-Up Purchases (buy when the job demands)

  • Hex/Torx bit sets (Mercedes loves both), E-Torx sockets
  • Fuel pressure gauge kit (essential for K-Jetronic work)
  • OBD1 blink-code tool or 38-pin scanner (R129) / dwell meter (W107)
  • Trim removal tools, hog-ring pliers (upholstery), dual-action polisher
  • Engine support bar or hoist rental — the day you do motor mounts
